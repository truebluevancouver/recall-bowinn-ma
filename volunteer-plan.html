How people enter the team

See campaign publicly→Volunteer page→Signup→Personal follow-up→Orientation→First activity→Active volunteer

Broad public channels

  • Campaign website with a prominent Volunteer button
  • 3–5 short public videos each week
  • Public campaign updates, photos and event notices
  • Permitted public information tables and events
  • QR codes on lawful campaign materials
  • Existing volunteers sharing the same public signup page organically

Make joining easy

  • One signup form for every source
  • Ask only for useful contact, availability and role information
  • Follow up promptly
  • Run one orientation every week
  • Give each newcomer a concrete first activity
  • Follow up after the first activity and schedule the next one

Month-by-month execution

September

18 → 35

Build the machine. Finish the volunteer database, appoint 2–3 coordinators, establish weekly orientation, make the volunteer link prominent and begin the repeatable content schedule.

October

35 → 57

Scale without chaos. Add coordinators as the team grows, maintain consistent public activity, and make sure every new signup is moved toward a real first activity.

November

57 → 78

Retention month. Identify people who have gone inactive, check availability, improve onboarding bottlenecks and keep recurring volunteers scheduled.

December

78 → 100–150

Continue broad public recruitment while protecting retention. Build capacity above 100 because not everyone will be available every week.

Volunteer team structure

Roles

  • Registered petition canvassers, when approved
  • Events and public information
  • Administration and data
  • Volunteer onboarding
  • Logistics and lawful sign/material delivery
  • Photography/video and general communications
  • Team coordinators

Management rule

As the campaign grows, aim for roughly one coordinator for every 8–12 active volunteers. The campaign manager should manage coordinators and systems instead of individually managing 100+ people.

Donations + compliance

Keep financial activity controlled by the financial agent.

For 2026, Elections BC lists the recall contribution limit as $1,513.29 per eligible individual to a proponent. Eligible contributors must be B.C. residents and Canadian citizens or permanent residents. Corporations, unions and organizations cannot make recall contributions. Cash contributions are limited to $100; contributions above $100 must use an approved non-cash method. Only the registered financial agent or an assistant financial agent may collect recall contributions and incur recall expenses.

Recall advertising is regulated. Paid/boosted online messages can trigger advertising-sponsor and authorization requirements. Do not casually boost campaign posts without first checking the applicable Elections BC requirements.

Compliance summary is operational guidance only. Confirm current requirements with Elections BC before spending, fundraising or advertising.

Recall canvassing rules to build into training

  • Only registered canvassers may collect recall signatures.
  • Canvassers must be volunteers and cannot be compensated for canvassing time.
  • Do not collect signatures until Elections BC has approved registration and issued the canvasser ID.
  • Petition sheets must not be left unattended; the canvasser must witness signatures.
  • Permission may be required for apartment buildings, strata property, malls, recreation centres, schools and other private/public facilities.
  • Follow applicable local bylaws, property rules and Elections BC instructions.
